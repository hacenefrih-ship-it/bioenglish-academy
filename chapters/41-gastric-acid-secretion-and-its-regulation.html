Gastric Acid Secretion and Its Regulation

University-level biology and scientific English lesson, with bilingual explanation and learning activities.

Open the interactive academyAll chapters

Scientific explanation — English

Parietal cells secrete hydrochloric acid into the gastric lumen through an apical hydrogen–potassium ATPase. Carbonic anhydrase generates hydrogen ions and bicarbonate from carbon dioxide and water. Bicarbonate exits across the basolateral membrane, producing a transient alkaline tide after meals. Gastrin, histamine and acetylcholine stimulate acid secretion through distinct receptors and signaling pathways. Somatostatin limits this response. The mucus–bicarbonate barrier helps protect the gastric epithelium from acid and pepsin. Acid facilitates protein denaturation and pepsin activation, but it does not replace pancreatic digestion in the small intestine.

Explication en français

Les cellules pariétales sécrètent l’acide chlorhydrique dans la lumière gastrique grâce à une ATPase hydrogène–potassium apicale. L’anhydrase carbonique produit des ions hydrogène et du bicarbonate à partir du dioxyde de carbone et de l’eau. Le bicarbonate sort par la membrane basolatérale, provoquant une alcalinisation sanguine transitoire après les repas. La gastrine, l’histamine et l’acétylcholine stimulent la sécrétion acide par des récepteurs et voies de signalisation distincts. La somatostatine limite cette réponse. La barrière mucus–bicarbonate protège l’épithélium gastrique contre l’acide et la pepsine. L’acidité facilite la dénaturation des protéines et l’activation de la pepsine, sans remplacer la digestion pancréatique intestinale.

Learning and communication advice

Self-assessment questions

  1. Which transporter directly releases hydrogen ions into the gastric lumen?

    H+/K+ ATPase / Na+/glucose cotransporter / Aquaporin-2

    Show answer and explanation

    H+/K+ ATPase — La pompe H+/K+ ATPase apicale échange les protons contre le potassium.

  2. Why may blood become transiently more alkaline after a meal?

    Pepsin enters the circulation / The stomach absorbs large amounts of ammonia / Bicarbonate exits parietal cells into blood

    Show answer and explanation

    Bicarbonate exits parietal cells into blood — La production d’acide s’accompagne d’une sortie basolatérale de bicarbonate.

  3. Which mediator generally inhibits gastric acid secretion?

    Gastrin / Somatostatin / Histamine

    Show answer and explanation

    Somatostatin — La somatostatine freine directement ou indirectement la sécrétion acide.

  4. A drug blocks histamine H2 receptors. What is the expected effect?

    Reduced acid secretion / Increased proton-pump abundance immediately / Complete cessation of pancreatic enzymes

    Show answer and explanation

    Reduced acid secretion — L’histamine stimule normalement la sécrétion via les récepteurs H2.
